ESAG

Earliest Stable Arrival Guidance for Explicit Stable-Completion Timing in Fixed-Horizon Diffusion Motion Planning

Method Results Real Robot

Abstract

Fixed-horizon diffusion motion planning can generate complete point-to-point trajectories over a prescribed temporal support, but existing terminal goal constraints mainly specify the conditions to be satisfied at the trajectory endpoint and do not explicitly regulate when the goal-position and low-velocity conditions begin to hold persistently. To address this issue, we propose Earliest Stable Arrival Guidance (ESAG), which defines stable task completion through the persistent satisfaction of the goal-position and low-velocity conditions over the remaining trajectory and treats the first time at which such persistent satisfaction is established as an explicit optimization quantity within the fixed horizon. We further construct a suffix-occupancy representation of the earliest stable-arrival time, prove its exact equivalence to the normalized stable-arrival time, and derive a differentiable ESAG objective through smooth approximations. The resulting objective is incorporated into the reverse-diffusion process as inference-time guidance without retraining the diffusion prior or modifying the planning horizon. Experiments on 60 held-out scenarios show that ESAG increases the stable-completion rate from 9.00% to 91.33% while largely preserving the navigation success rate. It also advances both goal-entry and stable-arrival times, shortens the path length, and maintains a 100% collision-free rate together with high motion feasibility. Real-robot experiments further show that ESAG increases the stable-completion rate from 13.33% to 95.00% and exhibits a consistent advancement in stable-arrival timing.
